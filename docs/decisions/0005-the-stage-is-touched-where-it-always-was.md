# 5. The stage is touched where it always was, not all at once afterwards

**What was decided.** The rules still steer the stage as they go: jump a
clip, put up a notice, ask for a sound, at the point in a frame where that
was always done. They do not gather up what they want done and have it done
at the end of the frame, and there is no list of things that have happened
for other parts to pick up later.

**Why.** Gathering up what is to be done and doing it afterwards is a good
way to keep sums apart from drawing, and it was the first thing tried on
paper. It does not fit this engine without changing the game. Everything
done to the stage goes into one queue in the order it was done, a new thing
takes the next free place on the stage, and both can be seen from outside.
Done afterwards, things would be done in another order.

**What was done to keep sums apart from drawing all the same.** The sums
were moved into functions that touch nothing on the stage and hand back a
small thing to be drawn, and the drawing stays where it was. Getting the
view ready for a pitch became a list of steps in the old order. Deciding
the pitch is one of them and touches nothing.

**What is left as it is, on purpose.** How far a runner has got is the
frame his clip is on, the ball leaves the pitcher's hand on a frame of his
wind-up, and where a fielder stands is read back from the stage. The art is
the clock, and keeping the time twice would be two things to keep in step.
