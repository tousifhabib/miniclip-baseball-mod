# 10. A tournament is made of full matches

**What was decided.** A tournament adds no kind of game. A fixture the
player is in is a full match, played on the full match's own screen by
the full match's own code, by other numbers: the tournament's innings, the
skill level it was drawn at, the other side's runs leant by its strength,
home or away as the draw has it, and a seed of its own. A fixture the
player is not in is a match played wholly on paper, both sides doing what
the other side of a full match does.

**What it might have been.** A fourth kind of game beside the last
innings, the arcade game and the full match, with a screen of its own.
The play asks which kind of game it is in some forty places, and the tests
and the record of whole games know a full match by the word the game says
of itself. A fourth kind would have gone through all of that to be a full
match in everything but its name.

**How the numbers get there.** The game a match is played by is already a
copy of the player's, with what the mods change of the numbers laid over
it. A fixture's numbers are laid over that copy in the same place. Nothing
in the play knows a tournament is on, and a full match by itself draws
the numbers it drew and touches the stage as it did.

**What a tournament keeps.** What was chosen for it, the sides in the
order they were drawn, and a card of each fixture played. The fixtures to
come, the tables, the winners and every figure are worked out from those
when they are asked for, so there is nothing to fall out of step. A card
is each side's runs innings by innings and the figures of its nine places
in the order. It is not the book: every pitch of fifteen matches is more
than is worth keeping, and where each ball came down, how each swing was
timed and the innings turn by turn are to be read when a match ends and
not after.

**Results are kept, not played again.** A fixture on paper comes out the
same for the same seed on one machine, but the sums the paper innings do
are the machine's own in the last place. So a result once reached is
written down in its card and never worked out a second time.

**Where it is read.** On the board the art has for an innings that was
tied, which a full match writes on between innings. The menu's panels are
too narrow for a table of batting, and the board's pages were written in
the board's own measures already, so the tables of batting, of figures
and of innings are written by one writer each for a full match and a
tournament alike.

**Whose the names are.** In a match the player's side is "you", as the
boards have always spoken, and the other side has its name. The player's
side goes by its own name only in the tournament's tables, where it is
one side among several.

**What it cost the record.** The seventh row of the menu's first page
moved the other six. The record's batter ends most games by pressing his
way to that page, so the record was written down afresh in the commit
that added the row. Everything before that commit left it as it was: the
tournament was built, and could be played from the command line, with
every game there had been still going frame for frame as it did.
